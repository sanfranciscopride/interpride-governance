## Adopted Text (`04.06.2026` Edition)

- **A.** PURPOSE AND SCOPE This policy supersedes all existing policies and or statements pertaining to Risk Management. The purpose of this Risk Policy is to provide a structured framework for identifying, assessing, mitigating, monitoring, and communicating risks that may affect InterPride’s ability to achieve its mission of advancing LGBT human rights globally. This policy applies to all InterPride activities, including governance, operations, programs, events, partnerships, and financial management.


- **B.** RISK IDENTIFICATION Risks will be identified through:
  - Regular risk review meetings at the Board and committee levels.
  - Input from members, staff/contractors, volunteers, and partners.
  - Monitoring of local, national, and international contexts, particularly in regions where InterPride works. Risk categories include:
  - Operational: Staff shortages, IT failures, event disruptions.
  - Financial: Fraud, funding shortfalls, exchange rate volatility, abuse of fiduciary responsibility duties, non- compliance with reporting requirements.
  - Reputational: Negative media coverage, community backlash.
  - Strategic: Shifts in legal landscapes, partner misalignment, political mayhem.
  - Legal & Compliance: Breach of laws, particularly U.S. (Texas) and international nonprofit law, data privacy, and anti-discrimination laws, failure to update this Interpride Risk Policy, failure to declare and or manage conflicts of interests per Interpride Conflicts of Interests Policy as updated.


- **C.** RISK ASSESSMENT Risks are assessed based on:
  - Likelihood (Rare, Unlikely, Possible, Likely, Almost Certain)
  - Impact (Insignificant, Minor, Moderate, Major, Catastrophic)
  - A Risk Matrix is used to prioritise and focus attention on high-risk areas.

- **D.** RISK MITIGATION InterPride employs the following strategies:
  - Preventive actions: Internal controls, staff training, cybersecurity protocols, clear Code of Ethics and Standing Rules, clear Conflicts of Interests Policy and or failure to manage any conflicts, real or perceived.
  - Contingency planning: Backup vendors for conferences, emergency communication plans, crisis management plans.
  - Insurance: Liability coverage, event insurance, Directors & Officers (D&O) insurance.
  - Decentralised operations: Reducing reliance on any one geography or system.
  - Board Risk & compliance training: set up a Risk Committee and or if not, make it a part of either Governance or Finance, and provide the necessary training.


- **E.** RISK MONITORING AND REVIEW Risks are reviewed quarterly by the Board and at each Mid-Year and General Meeting. A Risk Register will be maintained and updated by the Finance and Governance Committees. Emerging risks are flagged by staff and committees for immediate assessment.


- **F.** ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES Risks are assessed based on:
  - Board of Directors: Oversight and approval of the Risk Policy; reviews risk reports.
  - Officers: Operational implementation of risk mitigation strategies.
  - Committees: Assess risks specific to their mandates (e.g., Governance, Human Rights, Finance)
  - Staff and Contractors: Day-to-day identification and reporting of risks.
  - Members and Volunteers:
  - Encourage awareness and proactive communication

- **G.** REPORTING AND COMMUNICATION Risks and mitigation efforts are included in quarterly board reports and annual member updates. Urgent risks are escalated to the Co-Presidents immediately unless it involves a Co-President, in which case, to the Chairs of Governance Committee. All risk communication is conducted transparently and aligned with the Equal Opportunity and Respect policy.


- **H.** COMPLIANCE AND REVIEW This policy is reviewed annually by the Risk Management Committee and Governance Committee:
  - It complies with IRS guidelines for 501(c)(3) organizations, Texas nonprofit law, GDPR (for EU engagements), and applicable international human rights standards.
  - Updates must be approved by the Board and shared with all stakeholders.
  - All incoming Board members, staff, and committee chairs will receive training during onboarding.
  - Refresher sessions will be provided annually during the General Meeting & World Conference.
  - The policy is included in the Board Handbook, Code of Ethics, and referenced in the GAC and Staff Agreements.
