InterPride is a global network of 400+ Pride organizations from 70+ countries,
connecting and coordinating the efforts of all Prides on every continent.
Our work helps to further strengthen the global Pride movement, uniting
people from all walks of life to celebrate diversity and inclusion.
InterPride’s Mission is:
   ◊ To contribute to the visibility and inclusion of diverse sexual orientations, gender
       identities and expressions, and sex characteristics (SOGIESC) communities.
    ◊ To connect, grow, and support Pride platforms and networks globally.
    ◊ To assist the Pride movement to design and conduct human rights events that
       deconstruct myths, lies, and prejudices surrounding SOGIESC realities.
    ◊ To maximize networking and communication across the global Pride movement,
       offer resources, grow, and leverage partnerships.

     2024 was a transition year for both the Treasurer and Finance. After 8 years
     of dedicated service Dave Wait passed the baton of Treasurer to newly
     elected Mark Liberson. The Finance Committee had two new co-chairs,
     past Treasurers Dave Wait and Linda DeMarco joined the committee to
     help facilitate the transition. Along with providing the Board with monthly
     financials, most of the 1st quarter was spent transitioning accounts over
     into the new Treasurer’s name.
     As the year progressed, the Treasurer spent time working with the Conference
     Committee on the upcoming General Meeting & World Conference (GM&WC)
     in Medellin October 23–27, 2024, hosted by Corporación Stonewall. Funds
     were needed from the savings to secure hotel rooms. With no new Fund
     Development coming in, the decision was made to hire a professional Fund
     Developer. Efforts were put forward to hire this person by the end of summer
     with the hopes of getting sponsorships for the GM&WC as well as new funding.
     In addition, an updated travel policy was recommended by the finance
     committee and approved by the Board to put into the Standing Rules.
     With the hiring of the bookkeeper in 2023, financial transactions and records
     have become easily accessible on a regular basis not only to the Treasurer
     but to the Board as well. Working with the CPA firm as well as the Donor
     Solicitation firm, InterPride met its financial obligation as far as filings
     The 3rd quarter was met with financial challenges with the upcoming
     GM&WC. Meeting payment deadlines and negotiations of contract took
     up most of the Treasurers time. In addition, much time was spent with
     interviews for the Fund Development role. A candidate was selected and
     hired, however not able to start until right before the GM&WC therefore
     no new sponsorships and fundings was available. Members of the finance
     committee worked on a proposed budget for the Treasurer to present to
     the Board for ratification at the GM&WC.
     Finishing out the 4th and final quarter of the year found a lot more
     transition for the Finance Committee. Shortly after the end of the GM&WC
     in November, Treasurer Mark Liberson stepped down. The newly elected
     Co-Presidents appointed Dave Wait as interim Treasurer for 30-60 days
     until a new one could be appointed by the Board. Members of the Finance
     Committee stepped up to work with the newly hired Fund Developer and
     the bookkeeper continued working on making sure the financials were

     In 2024, I visited Prides in Guatemala and Honduras in Central America,
     Argentina and Uruguay in South America and Spain for the InterPride–
     EPOA Summit. As a result of my visits, these recommendations follow:
     Reduce Membership Fees for Prides in Countries Facing Economic Hardship
     I proposed a two-year pilot program (2025–2026) to offer a reduced
     membership fee of USD5 for Prides in countries and cities facing
     economic hardship. Financially stable Prides or companies could sponsor
     this reduced fee.
     First Time attendees and Scholarship Recipient Action Steps
     I developed a process for first time attendees and scholarship recipients
     at the General Meeting & World Conference (GM&WC) covering:
        •   Visa process information
        •   Information about the conference county/location, daily per diem,
             local emergency numbers and conference contact numbers.
        •    Designate a person to respond to questions before and during
              the conference.
        •     Send out a post-conference survey.
     Reconfiguration of InterPride Regions in Central and South America
     and the Caribbean
     In October 2024, together with Welton Trindale from Brazil, we proposed
     a reconfiguration of InterPride regions in Central and South America, and
     the Caribbean as follows:
     Region 1: All countries in Central and South Americas whose primary
               language is Spanish.
     Region 9: All Caribbean countries whose main language is Creole, Pidgin
                English, Patuá, French, Dutch, and/or English.
     Region 17: Made up of Brazil, whose primary language is Portuguese.
     This reconfiguration will further energize the regional work with our
     organizations and will allow each region to participate and be heard from
     its particular realities.

     PRIDE PLATFORMS
     EUROPEAN PRIDE ORGANISERS’ ASSOCIATION (EPOA)
     In 2024, the European Pride Organisers Association (EPOA) made significant
     strides in advancing LGBTQ+ human rights across Europe. One of the most
     notable achievements was the selection of Torino, Italy, as the host city
     for EuroPride 2027. This decision, made during EPOA’s Annual General
     Meeting in Porto, Portugal, sent a strong message to the Italian government
     regarding the importance of LGBTQ+ rights and visibility.
     Throughout the year, EPOA remained responsive to incidents affecting
     the LGBTQ+ community. In March 2024, following a transphobic attack in
     Thessaloniki, Greece, EPOA issued a statement emphasizing the importance
     of promoting visibility and solidarity and combatting discrimination and
     violence against LGBTQ+ individuals.
     EPOA proactively engaged with international bodies. In evidence published
     in January 2024 to the UN Independent Expert on sexual orientation and
     gender identity Dr Graeme Reid, EPOA confirmed that their members are
     reporting increasing attacks on Pride events and individuals.
     EuroPride 2024 in Thessaloniki, Greece
     A comprehensive program of activities was planned and delivered by
     Thessaloniki Pride from 21 to 29 June, including a human rights conference
     with participation of Council of Europe Commissioner for Human Rights
     Michael O’Flaherty and the European Commissioner for Equality Helena
     Dalli. The EuroPride 2024 parade was the culmination of a week-long
     series of events celebrating diversity and advocating for equal rights.
     Annual General Meeting (AGM) 2024
     The AGM took place from November 1-3, 2024, in Porto, Portugal, hosted
     by EPOA member Variações. Delegates from across Europe convened for
     plenaries, workshops, and networking sessions and focussed the future
     direction of EPOA, the broader Pride movement, and strategies to counteract
     rising hostility.
     EuroPride 2027 Host Selection
     Four cities — Turin (Italy), Vilnius (Lithuania), Torremolinos (Spain), and
     Gloucestershire (United Kingdom) — submitted bids for selection for
     EuroPride 2027. Torino Pride secured 53% of the votes, earning the right
     to host EuroPride 2027.

     GENERAL MEETING &
     WORLD CONFERENCE
     MEDELLIN, COLOMBIA, SOUTH AMERICA
     InterPride’s General Meeting & World Conference took place in Medellín,
     Colombia, from 23 to 27 October, 2024 — marking the first time the event
     was held in South America.
     Prior to the conference, the Board allocated 50 free places for Pride
     members from South America. This was done to offset financial barriers
     and challenges faced by certain organizations. It was gratifying, therefore,
     to see a significant number of South Americans among the over 200
     conference attendees.
     The conference was excellently organized by the host organization,
     Corporación Stonewall, with a large number of local volunteers providing
     valuable assistance. The evening social events, including the conference
     dinner, were lively and showcased the vibrant local culture.
     Exciting updates were shared regarding upcoming WorldPride events in
     Washington, D.C. (2025) and Amsterdam (2026). The two cities vying for
     WorldPride 2028 — Guadalajara, Mexico, and Cape Town, South Africa —
     both made compelling presentations. In the voting by member organizations
     following the conference, Cape Town was selected as the first WorldPride
     event to be held in Africa and the second in the Southern Hemisphere.
     One plenary session featured a vigorous discussion on the criteria for
     Board positions—specifically, whether candidates needed to have attended
     a previous conference. Prior to the event, the Board had amended the
     rules to allow those active in InterPride committees to run for the Board,
     aiming for greater inclusivity. However, legal counsel overruled this change,
     stating it needed a vote from members. As a result, the Board postponed
     the election for three positions until the proposed change could be voted
     on by members. The membership overwhelmingly supported the change,
     with over 80% voting in favor. The elections for the Board positions were
     then held.
     Other significant motions passed in the final plenary and confirmed by
     members included setting aside a pre-conference day for BIPOC and Trans
     programming, supporting the People Living with HIV Caucus’s request for
     InterPride to implement the Undetectable Equals Untransmittable (U=U)
     Global Campaign, and calling for a demographic and skills audit of Board
     members. Additionally, these motions provide voices and spaces for
     marginalized groups within the organization and to increase accountability
     to both the InterPride Board and its membership.

     MADRID SUMMIT
     INTERPRIDE AND EPOA LEADERSHIP MEETING
     In 2024, InterPride deepened its commitment to regional collaboration
     through a historic Leaders Summit with the European Pride Organisers
     Association (EPOA) in Madrid. This milestone event marked a new chapter
     in global Pride cooperation, culminating in the formal signing of a Pride
     Platform Agreement — strengthening shared advocacy efforts and uniting
     visions for the future of our movement.
     Following the October 2023 InterPride General Meeting & World
     Conference in San Diego, USA, Juan Carlos Alonso Reguero of MADO
     Madrid Pride extended an invitation to host a summit between InterPride
     and EPOA. This gathering took place from April 19 to 21, 2024, as EPOA
     was preparing its application to become a Pride Platform member of
     InterPride.
     Board members from both organisations convened in Madrid, with the
     majority attending in person and some joining virtually. After a warm
     welcome from the City of Madrid, participants engaged in dynamic and
     strategic conversations focused on shared goals, movement solidarity,
     and the evolving landscape of Pride organising worldwide. The summit
     was a rare opportunity for both Boards to reflect, align strategies, and
     strengthen relationships grounded in a shared commitment to LGBTQI+
     equality and justice.
     The meeting concluded with a significant milestone: EPOA officially
     signed the Pride Platform Agreement with InterPride, formalising a deeper
     collaboration between the two networks.
     Special appreciation goes to MADO Madrid Pride for generously hosting
     the summit. We especially thank Joseph Duggan Lyons for his facilitation
     and coordination, and Juan Carlos Alonso Reguero for his vision and
     initiative in making this historic gathering a reality.

     GRANTS AND HUMANITARIAN AID
     The 2024 Grants Committee was co-chaired by Andrea Gilbert (Athens
     Pride) and Alan Reiff (NYC Pride). Affectionately known as the “A-Team,”

     verification process, we awarded nine global grants.

     parade permits, and sound systems. One grant also ensured that a Pride
     organization and its leaders avoided bankruptcy and legal persecution
     following last-minute policy changes by their country’s Presiden. All grants
     were awarded to organizations in the Global South and Eastern Europe,
     regions that traditionally face significant funding barriers.
     At the World Conference & General Meeting in Medellín, the Grants
     Committee received over $24,000 USD in donations from Pride organizations
     and individuals, both in-person and online. These funds will be used

     RACISM & OPPRESSION AWARENESS PROGRAM (ROAP)
     Launched in October 2021, ROAP represents our focused effort to
     address racism and oppression within the LGBTQIA+ community and
     Pride organizations. The program includes:
         • InterPride Anti-Racism Statement
         • Educational webinars and TED Talk-style sessions
         • Curated reading materials and resources
         • Social media campaigns featuring stories and videos from
            diverse communities.
     Through ROAP, we encourage our member organizations to:
        •   Participate in educational programming
        •   Create safer spaces for Black, Indigenous, and People of Color
            (BIPOC) communities
        •   Implement anti-racism and anti-oppression practices
        •   Engage in discussions about unconscious bias, prejudice,
            microaggressions, colorism, and colonialism.


     GLOBAL TRANSGENDER TASK FORCE (TTF)
     The InterPride Global Transgender Task Force (TTF) was established
     following the last general meeting’s Trans caucus, bringing together over
     50 global members. With a clear mission and vision, the task force aims to
     provide essential resources and support for the transgender community,
     fostering a safe and inclusive environment.
     Why the Task Force is Needed
     The TTF addresses critical issues such as violence, legislative challenges,
     and the overall wellbeing of the global transgender community. Additionally,
     it ensures that transgender individuals feel safe and represented within
     the InterPride Board.
     Mission & Vision
     The task force advocates for transgender rights, working to improve
     quality of life and celebrate transgender lives globally.

     WORLDPRIDE 2025
     WASHINGTON, DC, USA
     WorldPride Washington, DC 2025, produced by the Capital Pride Alliance,
     united more than 1.2 million participants across 23 unforgettable days
     of visibility, culture, and activism from May 17 – June 8, 2025. The event
     coincided with the 50th anniversary of Pride in Washington, DC, and
     carried the theme “The Fabric of Freedom,” symbolizing the interwoven
     diversity and resilience of LGBTQ+ communities worldwide.
     Throughout the city, WorldPride DC showcased a remarkable blend of joy and
     celebration through events such as the free 2-day Street Festival and Parade,
     which stood out as the heart of the celebration in front of the U.S. Capitol
     Building. The Human Rights Conference, which convened global advocates
     from 30+ countries and over 1,400 attendees, stood as a beacon of advocacy
     and activism.
     The Capital Pride Alliance awarded $1.4 million in community grants and
     fostered new partnerships across civic, corporate, and grassroots sectors.
     The collaboration among over 60 agencies and 700 volunteers ensured the
     event remained inclusive and safe.
     Ultimately, WorldPride 2025 was more than a celebration — it stood as
     a powerful global declaration of resilience, love, and freedom in the face
     of significant political challenges.
     To view the full impact of WorldPride 2025: www.capitalpride.org/wp-impact
