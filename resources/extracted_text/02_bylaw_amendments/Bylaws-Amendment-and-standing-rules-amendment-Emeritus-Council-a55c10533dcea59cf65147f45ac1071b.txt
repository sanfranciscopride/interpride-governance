Proposed amendment

As proposers of his by-law amendment, we recognise the value for organisational
development in maintaining relations and contacts with former board members,
and therefore support the creation of an Emeritus Council. And we propose this in
the spirit of democracy, respect and progress.

We fully recognize the knowledge and experience that is contained in people
  with experience and long term service. They are valued members of our
  community and we are grateful for their service and continued commitment to
  our work.


This is not a proposal in opposition to an emeritus council, but in opposition to
   the introduction of a board position that is not rooted in democratic practices
   and accountability.


As it is set up now, the co-presidents appoint people to the emeritus council and
   from their midst the council are afforded the right to directly appoint a voting
   board member. No-where along this process is there ever a vote by the
   members on this board position.


We have in the years, that we have been part of InterPride, participated in
  discussions on how to attract new, young voices to our work and make it
  seem worth their while to spend time and effort in our association. The most
  important incentive, from our point of view, is that they feel, that they have
  genuine influence and are recognized on par with everyone else.


Maintaining a non-elected board position for a senior person, we perceive as an
  attempt to uphold influence on the organization, and not allow young activists
  full and unhindered access to shape InterPride according to their ideas and
  aspirations. If we look at the average age of the current board, it does not
   appear as if, we have a problem with maintaining experienced people, but a
   challenge with recruiting new and younger.


Advice is something you offer and not something you can force on people.


An emeritus council can exist, and offer advice to the board, which the board can
  seek when needed. This does not require a seat on the board or a vote.


Within the democratic structure of InterPride, any member – including seniors -
   are free to run for positions on the board if they wish, and be elected if the
   membership so desires.
As appointees of the Co-Presidents, however, the Emeritus Council should not
have the right to appoint a board member nor to have a vote on the board. The
Board are welcome to invite all or some of the Emeritus Council members to attend
board meetings to give counsel or for other purposes.

We therefore propose that the by-laws be amended to remove the appointed
  member of the emeritus council from the board.




Amend bylaws Article V, Section 1, to delete ‘Up to one (1) Emeritus Council
(voting) member’.

In addition, amend Standing Rule 01.03 ‘Emeritus Council’ to henceforth read as
follows:
