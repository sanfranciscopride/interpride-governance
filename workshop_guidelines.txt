Conference Committee

Workshop Proposal Guidelines 
InterPride’s General Meeting & World Conference is the largest annual gathering of international Pride organizers in the world. Our GM&WC provides opportunities for our global network of Pride organizers to connect with each other, learn new skills, and build powerful connections and knowledge, all in the service of continuing to grow the power of our global Pride movement.


Please review these guidelines before submitting your workshop proposal. 


________________




Registration Fees, Travel, and Lodging 
Presenters are responsible for the cost of registration, travel, and lodging. 


Registration fees help cover nearly 50% of the hard costs of producing the InterPride conference, and are an investment in the continuing legacy of our organization and our Pride movement. All presenters whose proposals are accepted for scheduling will receive a link to register at the discounted presenter rate. Presenters whose submissions are not accepted will receive a link to a registration rate equivalent to the regular member registration rate. 


All potential presenters are welcome and encouraged to register early to take advantage of the early-bird registration rate. After the early-bird deadline, please wait to register until you receive notice regarding your proposal so that you register at the correct rate. 


Presenters are eligible to apply for scholarships, but please note that the scholarship and presenter selection processes are entirely separate. That is: presenters are not more or less likely to receive a scholarship for attendance.


________________




Conference Schedule and Location 
All workshop programming will be held from 29 October - 1 November, 2026 at the Conference Hotel in Phuket, Thailand. Presenters are expected to plan travel and lodging according to the conference dates above. Session scheduling requests will be considered, but may not be able to be accommodated.


________________




Language Justice and Accessibility
InterPride is committed to ensuring language justice and accessibility for both attendees and presenters. 


We strive to provide access to content in multiple spoken languages and sign language, and may utilize appropriate and native-speaker-approved technological solutions to achieve our language justice goals. 


Our Accessibility team works closely within our Conference Committee to provide support to presenters and speakers to create accessible materials and content in their sessions. If your session is accepted, you will receive guidelines and support from the Accessibility team, which may include auditing of your materials for accessibility. 


Presenters with access needs will have an opportunity to identify those needs in their proposals and/or address those directly with our Accessibility upon acceptance of their session.

Content Priorities 
The InterPride Conference Committee seeks to curate workshop programming that provides a balance of sessions that provide practical tools and logistics with sessions that highlight the diversity of the international Pride movement and intersectional issues facing the LGBTQIA+ global communities. In addition, up to 20% of the workshop content at an in-person conference are reserved to focus on local and regional issues facing the local Pride movement.


Workshop proposals should reflect and support InterPride’s mission: InterPride exists to uplift the global Pride movement through: 1. Amplifying visibility and inclusion for underrepresented voices, especially in the Global South. 2. Strengthening networks among Pride organisers through intercultural collaboration and shared learning. 3. Equipping members with tools and knowledge for advocacy, fundraising, and event delivery. 4. Driving global advocacy by supporting member-led campaigns and leveraging InterPride’s status in international spaces. 5. Promoting financial resilience across the Pride movement through resource sharing and funding support.


Given the current global social and political climate, the Conference Committee prioritizes proposals that focus on:
* innovative approaches to Pride organising
* broadly applicable logistical tools or skills
* trans community
* Indigenous peoples
* communities of color / racialized communities
* disability justice
* Pride organisers in the Global South and/or in hostile or criminalized locations


________________




Workshop Format 
All workshops are scheduled for 60 minutes. Workshops that feature interactive and engaging content and clear learning objectives will be prioritized over lecture-only sessions.


________________




Learning Objectives
Workshop proposals are reviewed for emphasis on engagement and practice. On the submission form, you will be asked to respond to the following prompts:
* What will participants know, feel, or be able to do after your session? 
* What interactive strategies will you employ to actively engage participants and meet the objectives of your workshop? 
* What are you prioritizing: sharing knowledge, sharing tools, building skills, or all the above? 
* Is your session applicable to all, or specific to attendees with advanced knowledge or skill?

Descriptions of Tracks & Subcategories:


Pride Event & Organizational Operations
Sessions in this track will focus on the logistical side of Pride organizing and/or event production. This may include innovative tactics, best practices, and knowledge sharing related to funding and financial longevity, volunteer management, leadership development, communications, event production logistics. This track also includes the arts and culture component of many Pride events.
Subcategories:
* Arts & Culture
* Funding and Financial Stability
* Innovation
* InterPride - Operations & Organisation
* Logistics & Operations
* Marketing & Communications
* Rainbow-washing (Issues with non-authentic forms of support)


2SLGBTQIA+ Human Rights
Sessions in this track will create opportunities to share knowledge, skills, and tools related to the ongoing fight for 2SLGBTQIA+ human rights around the globe. These sessions are relevant to the current global social and political climate, and will create space for discussions around the connection between Pride organisations and intersectional social justice and liberation for our 2SLGBTQIA+ communities. 
Subcategories:
* Advocacy and Community Organizing
* Conversion Therapy / Sexual Orientation Change Efforts (SOCE)
* Criminalization & Discrimination 
* Legal Equality (Government/Policy)
* Migration / Immigration


Building an Inclusive Pride Movement
Sessions in this track will focus on building a Pride movement that is truly inclusive of all members of our diverse 2SLGBTQIA+ community. Sessions will build knowledge and understanding and provide tangible skills and tools for Pride organizers to do our intersectional work within the global Pride movement. Knowledge, skills, and tools shared may focus on the individual, interpersonal, organizational, governmental, or systemic level.
Subcategories:
* Disability Justice / Accessibility
* Elders / Seniors
* Faith, Spirituality, and Religion
* Inclusive Feminism
* Intersectionality
* Gender Justice: Justice for Trans, Non-Binary, Gender Non-Conforming, and Intersex People
* Global Majority (Global South) Perspectives
* Leather, Kink, & Alternative Sexuality Communities
* Racial Justice
* Youth


Healthy and Thriving 2SLGBTQIA+ Movements and Organizations
Sessions in this track focus on the broad range of physical and mental health issues critical to 2SLGBTQIA+ communities. This may include discussions of medical and cultural practices, health interventions, sexual health and liberation, resilience and resistance practices at individual or organizational levels, and ways that Pride organisers can engage in opportunities and activities to build toward better health outcomes for 2SLGBTQIA+ individuals and communities.
Subcategories:
* Healing Justice
* Health and Wellness
* HIV/AIDS
* Mental Health
* Relationship & Interpersonal Violence
* Sexual Health

No Solicitation 
Proposals that primarily promote or sell commercial products – and/or that promote or sell the commercial work of individual people – will be declined. This includes proposals that are primarily or tangentially intended to promote WorldPride bids.


________________




WorldPride Bidders - Workshops
The workshop proposal process is open to all, which includes all organizations bidding to host future WorldPrides. The Conference Committee will not factor a WorldPride bid status into the decision-making process for workshops; that is, WorldPride bidders will not receive preferential treatment or consideration in the review process. WorldPride bidding organizations are welcome to submit workshop proposals through the same process as other workshop proposals.


________________




Workshops from Sponsors
Sponsorship packages may include up to one (1) guaranteed workshop slot upon agreement from Conference Committee Co-Chairs. This workshop must adhere to all guidelines in this document, including the No Solicitation guideline.


________________




Proposal Review and Notification
A team from InterPride’s Conference Committee will review all proposals. Proposals are evaluated based on a rubric that includes the following: 
* Strength of Proposal (General): Proposal is clear and strong, particularly re: learning objectives, structure, and elements of audience engagement.
* Relevance to Global Pride Movement: The presentation is framed to be relevant to Pride organizers from around the world (i.e. not solely relevant to Pride organizers from any one country or region)
* Representation: The presenter(s) represent the community being discussed
* Alignment with InterPride's Values: Trust, Respect, Accountability, Diversity, Multiculturalism, Kindness, and Bravery
* Expertise: The presenter(s) are subject matter experts in the topic being presented through lived experience, education, profession, or other.


Every year, we receive more proposals than can be accommodated in the conference schedule, and we typically approve between 40-50% of proposals. Consider proactively reaching out to other subject matter experts in your field to combine your efforts and submit one joint workshop proposal.
