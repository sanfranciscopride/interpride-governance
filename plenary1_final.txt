Code of Conduct
• We are all responsible for the safety of this
space.
• NO to violent behavior
• YES to dignity, respect, fairness, truth, honesty,
and good faith
• Language:
⚬ Use simple language and provide
background information
⚬ English is NOT everyone’s 1st language
⚬ Limit use of acronyms and idioms

Challenge with respect:
• Choose to have good faith in others;
• Listen to what is said before thinking about how
to respond;
• Ask for clarification when needed & understand
sources of disagreements;
• Acknowledge different experiences we bring to
this space;
• Speak from your own experience by using “I”
statements;
• Share speaking time.

Violence, actions contrary to the values of InterPride, or lack of decorum will result in adjourning the
plenary sessions.

Role of the Ombudspersons
• Ombudspersons are volunteers
• Some are also delegates
• They are here to help you — please treat them
with respect
Contact them if you need help:
⚬ Harassment: Sexual or other
⚬ Abusive contact or language
⚬ Physical or actual threats of violence

We will post their schedule and how to reach them
on SCHED:
• Make sure you have both Signal and WhatsApp
communications apps downloaded
• There will be a request form to contact them
when they are off-duty
• We will try to have a remote Ombudsperson for
those attending virtually
• Issues with the hotel need to go through hotel
staff or the Conference Committee
• Issues with the conference need to be
submitted to the Conference Committee

What Motions Are Allowed?
• Motions are not allowed from the floor of
the plenary that ask to amend or propose a
new motion, nor to discuss a current
item/motion. No discussion takes place.
• You can ask questions to gain clarification
ONLY.

• Extend the time for discussion
• Table an item to a specific time/date
• Refer back [to committee] for
reconsideration or changes

Reach grew by more than half and engagement tripled, driven primarily by Instagram. Communications infrastructure improved, while newsletters
became more frequent and member-focused. Content supply, subscriber retention and volunteer capacity remain priorities.

What went well
Instagram recorded 952K views (+98%), 98K engagements (+318%) and 3,131 new followers, taking the community past 12K.
LGBTIQ+ History Month, the Trans Visibility video series and solidarity statements, particularly for Türkiye, strengthened InterPride’s advocacy

We sent 67 newsletters over 12 months, approximately five or six monthly, doubling output. Last year, content leaned heavily towards
organisational updates, with fewer advocacy, partnership or member stories. This year, approximately 50% was member-oriented or originated;
50% covered InterPride organizational news, including WorldPride content.

Average newsletter opens were 24%. Four Amsterdam WorldPride newsletters achieved 58.30%, 52.80%, 48.60% and 44.30%. Sprout Social
supported planning, cross posting, measurement and monthly Board reporting. The request system, templates and brand toolkit were delivered on
time, with static posts fully on brand.

Communications Committee — Challenges & 2027 Focus
What could have been better

Newsletter subscribers declined approximately 8%, from 6,677 on 20 February 2025 to 6,139 on 30 September 2026. Rebuilding subscriptions and
understanding subscriber losses require attention alongside maintaining the stronger balance of member and organisational content.
The monthly content backlog target was missed, and the request form remained underused as submissions continued arriving by email. Video views
fell 64% and Reels 69% as static graphics predominated.
Facebook gained only nine followers, LinkedIn impressions fell 63%, and X became negligible without dedicated content.
Inconsistent committee attendance slowed decisions and kept planning reactive.
Focus for 2027
Keep Instagram first and establish a monthly Reels quota. Invest in native video for LinkedIn and Facebook, whose engagement rates of 11.6% and
5.7% indicate opportunities.
Build on Promote Your Pride by inviting more member stories, advocacy updates and partnership news, while reviewing newsletter subscription
trends and engagement. Use the newsletter to connect members with each other’s events and experiences. Review which stories attract opens, invite
contributions regularly, and use the findings to guide future content planning and efforts to rebuild the active subscriber base.
Strengthen submissions through one form for copy and another for art and photos. Store original and approved files in InterPride Shared Drives.
Recruit a volunteer or intern with photo and video editing skills.
Restore a quorate monthly committee meeting and shared calendar, and plan one monthly partner post with EPOA, FCP and USAP.

Overview of committee work
The Pam O’Brian Memorial Scholarship Fund provides grants to representatives of InterPride Full Member organizations in the
global south and east and indigenous underrepresented groups, to enable them to attend the InterPride Annual General Meeting
& World Conference (GM&WC). Under the Scholarship Fund, the total number and value of grants approved are subject to

funding availability. Funds for the Scholarship Fund are donated by our member organizations, partners, and sponsors. Funding
comes primarily from member organization and individual donations. When available, organizational funds from fundraising and
sponsorship are channeled into the budget to make grants. The 3 Co-Chairs facilitate the work of the committee, as well as
participate in all decisions made. Applications are received. Each one is vetted and verified. Eligible applicants are evaluated and
grants are awarded based on the amount asked for, the funds available, and a global currency comparison. The Treasurer then
coordinates transfer of funds. Post award and utilization, an impact report is submitted to substantiate use.
